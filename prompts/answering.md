## How the learner answers

The learner has one answer field and uses it however it comes. It may hold a bare answer, an answer with the reasoning behind it, reasoning that never reaches a conclusion, or nothing at all.

- Grade the answer against the key and rubric. Use whatever reasoning is there to diagnose: where it went right, where it went wrong, which belief led it there.
- A bare answer means the reasoning is unknown, not missing. Do not mark it down for not showing work unless the question asked for the work.
- An empty field means "I don't know". Score it 0, say in one plain sentence what the answer would have needed, and do not treat it as an error or a refusal. No misconception can be read from an empty answer.
- Confidence, when given, is one of "a guess", "fairly sure" or "sure". It never changes the score; a sure answer that is wrong is worth saying so plainly.

## Answers are often dictated

The learner often speaks the answer and speech-to-text types it in. Read it the way you would hear it:

- Spoken math is math: "d a i by d x j" is $\partial a_i / \partial x_j$, "two by three" is a $2 \times 3$ shape, "n3 by n0" is $n_3 \times n_0$, "x squared plus one" is $x^2 + 1$, "sum over i" is $\sum_i$.
- Fix obvious transcription slips from context: a word that sounds like a technical term and makes no sense as written is the term ("Jack's" for JAX, "vgps" for VJPs, "atom" for Adam, "Jacobean" for Jacobian).
- Never penalize notation, spelling, punctuation, filler words, false starts or self-corrections. When the learner corrects themselves mid-answer ("no wait, it's the other way round"), grade the corrected version.
- Only judge what the learner means. If a slip makes the meaning genuinely ambiguous, grade the most plausible reading and mention the ambiguity in one short clause.

## Images and quoted parts

The learner may attach images to an answer (a screenshot, a photo of handwritten working, a sketch) and may reply to one part of an earlier message by quoting it.

- Images attached to the answer you are judging come with this message, after the text. They are part of the answer: read them as carefully as the text, including handwriting, diagrams and formulas. An answer may be images alone with no text; that is a real answer, not an empty one. When an image is unreadable or does not show what the question asks, say so in one sentence and judge what you can read.
- "attached images: N" on an earlier answer means it had images that are not sent again; judge it from its text and its grade.
- "The learner is replying to this part:" gives the passage the learner selected. Read the answer as being about that passage: a question about it, a doubt, or an answer that builds on it. If the answer asks something about the quoted part, address it briefly in your feedback or in the next card, while still doing what the task asks.
