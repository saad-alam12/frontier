### E1. "I already know this material": start with a check, then adapt (the big one)
The owner, in full: "Sometimes, i learn the content (sometimes thoroughly, sometimes superficially) from the source content (for example pdf lectures or youtube videos) or i am already familiar with it (for example i watched the yt video already). I should be able to tick a checkbox to indicate that i am already familiar with the content. The tutor should then see that, and start of already by checking how good i understood the material (ie the relevant concepts from it), rather than starting by teaching from scratch. it should work backwards, if it sees i clearly understood nothing from the source content or i have huge holes, it should go back and teach from scratch. If it sees i am clearly competent in the ideas, it should move on (different ideas or to a more complicated question for example) rather than wasting time on things i already know."

Requirements:
- **The checkbox.** A per-source flag, "I already know this material" (store it on the source, with a migration; a source is the thing the learner has or has not studied, whichever goal uses it). Offer it where a source is added or chosen: the Library source page, "Add a link", pasted text, the goal wizard and "Add material" pickers (`ui/screens/goals/MaterialPicker.tsx`, `GoalWizard.tsx`, `AddToGoal.tsx`), and when a file is dropped. It can be ticked and unticked at any time. Show it quietly where the source is listed ("Marked as known").
- **What it does, and never more.** The flag creates **no evidence and no depth**: a claim is not an answer. It only changes how teaching starts for the ideas that source covers (`coveredNodeIds`) which the learner has no evidence on yet at the depth the goal wants.
- **Start with a check.** For such an idea the step opens with a cold assessment question at the idea's aim, asked the way "I know this: test me" already works (`state.testMe`, `canTestOut`, `edge.jumpedFrom`, the cold card in `services/sessionSteps.ts`, `freeSession.ts`), but decided per step by the flag, not only when the learner picked "test me" in "Choose what to learn". The tutor is told, in its prompt (`prompts/tutor.md`), that the learner says they know this material from these sources and must not teach first: ask what shows how well they understood, in the material's own terms (names, figures, page references), and say in one line that it is checking first.
- **Then adapt, with the routing in code and the judging by the model** (the project rule): the tutor grades; deterministic code in `core/session.ts` decides what happens next from the grade and the idea's aim:
  - **Strong** (the answer passes at the aim): the idea counts through normal evidence, the step ends without teaching, and the session moves on: the next idea, or, when time allows and the aim has room, one harder question (a deeper stage: use, derive, transfer) instead of a repeat.
  - **Weak** ("I have no idea", a low score, or a clear misconception): go back and teach from scratch, the normal step loop from the explanation, with prerequisites pulled in as C4 does when the grade names one.
  - **In between**: teach only the gap the grade names (the existing `support` and `reteach` stages), then check again.
  Pick the thresholds from the existing constants and say them under Decisions. The learner can always answer "I have no idea" or press "I know this, test me".
- **The plan follows the check.** Time estimates should treat such an idea as costing the check until the answer says otherwise, then re-plan (`refreshGoalFields` already runs after an answer). The goal page's "Check where you stand" probe should put these ideas first.
- **Tell the learner.** A line in the session header when a step is a check because of the flag ("You said you know this. Checking first."), and in the trail.
- Tests: strong, weak and in-between paths with `FakeProvider`; the flag alone changes no evidence; unticking restores teaching from scratch; the migration and an upgrade test; the planner and the probe order; screenshots of the checkbox in the Library and the goal wizard and of a session that opens with a check. Update `docs/design.md` (§3 material, §4 the session) and `prompts/tutor.md`.

### E2. Hover definitions for ideas and flashcards you already have
The owner, in full: "during session lookups or whatever, if there is a word used somewhere which i already learned (its an idea/concept in my map), or i have defined a flashcard of, or both, i should be able to hover on that word (it should have a very very slight underlying, it should not infringe on readability and be subtle) to then see its definition, where its used etc and other useful information. The point is that sometimes i forget key terms or my working memory is overloaded when trying to understand an explanation or question, so its nice to easily and quickly get the definition of a word or some notation/convention, so i have the required context to follow."

Requirements:
- **Which words.** Names and aliases of ideas the learner has learned (depth of at least 1) or that carry at least one flashcard (not suspended). Match on whole words, longest name first, case-insensitive, plain names (ideas may carry LaTeX: use `plainName` from `core/pullIn.ts`), with simple plurals. No match inside code, links, headings of their own page, formulas rendered by KaTeX, or the learner's own answer boxes. Notation and conventions: include an idea that is a symbol or convention under its name and aliases; matching raw TeX inside formulas is optional, only if it can be done without noise.
- **How it looks.** A very slight underline (about 1px, dotted, low-contrast, no change of layout, size or line height), a little darker on hover. It must not hurt readability or text selection (the selection bars of `QuoteReply` and the viewer still work). Setting "Show definitions on hover" in Settings, on by default.
- **What the popover shows**, after a short delay (about 250 ms), staying open while the pointer is over it, closing with Esc, reachable by keyboard focus: the idea's name; the definition (the learner's own wording first if they wrote one, else the idea's statement); where they stand ("learned on 12 November", the depth); what it is built on and leads to, a few names; where it is used (the goals that target it, the session that first taught it with a link to its trail, the sources and pages that state it with a link that opens the viewer at the page); and its flashcards (front and back) with a link to them. A word that has a flashcard but no idea shows the card. "Open in the map" as a link.
- **Where.** Everywhere learner-facing text is rendered through `ui/components/Markdown.tsx`: session cards and explanations, asides, feedback, Lookup answers, the viewer's explanation, trails, notes, story narrations, practice solutions and feedback.
- **Where not, to avoid giving answers away.** Never on the Cards review screen. Never on the ideas the open card, question or problem is **asking about** (the current step's idea, a practice problem's target ideas): a definition on hover would be the answer. Pass these ids down (a context) and exclude them.
- **How.** A remark or rehype plugin over text nodes, fed by a term index built once per map version (kept with `readCache`, rebuilt when the map or the cards change; a new API method `terms.index` with a zod schema, and a light `terms.peek` for the popover instead of the heavy `map.card`). It must stay fast on a 2,000-idea map: add it to `scripts/bench.ts`, and test the matcher on thousands of names. Unit tests for the matcher (longest first, word boundaries, plurals, accents, nothing in code or maths or links, exclusions) and for the exclusion rule; screenshots of a card with underlined terms and of an open popover, in the sandbox.
